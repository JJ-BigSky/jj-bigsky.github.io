01 // The long version

This sheet is being drafted.The reality-gap meter needs a real needle.

The long version sorts the three things people call a world model, explains why your CAD is not a simulation asset, and gives you a meter that says how much of a given task sim can honestly do. Until it's up, our position, stated plainly so you can disagree with it:

  • Physics simulation is where your budget goes today. GPU-parallel rigid-body simulation running thousands of environments at once. Mature, boring in the good way.
  • Learned world models are where your data augmentation goes next. Video world models are genuinely impressive and not yet a training environment for your cell. A model that predicts plausible pixels is not predicting your friction coefficient.
  • Anyone telling you latent world models are production-ready for contact-rich manipulation is describing a roadmap, not a product.
  • The gap is task-shaped. Locomotion and rigid pick-and-place transfer well. Contact-rich assembly and deformables transfer badly. That's knowable before you build the environment, and it should determine how much you build.

Our read of the published evidence and the physics, as of September 2026.

SKY-1 says: the digital twin is doing great. The physical one has a loose cable.

02 // What we do

Four things, measured against the real cell.A modest simulator you have honestly characterised beats a beautiful one you trust.

  • Sim environment build. From your CAD or scanned from your actual cell, with an asset pipeline that survives the next revision.
  • Randomization & system ID. A documented plan for what varies in training and a measured match between simulated and real dynamics.
  • Sim-versus-real harness. The same task, scored the same way, in both places, tracked over time, so “the gap” is a number and not a feeling.
  • Scenario & rare-event coverage. The failure cases you can't safely stage on a real line, generated and tested where they're free.
Your CAD is not a simulation asset. Decimation, convex decomposition, collision geometry, joint definitions, mass and inertia that aren't defaults. Doing it once is a project. Doing it every time the design changes is a pipeline, and the pipeline is the deliverable.
